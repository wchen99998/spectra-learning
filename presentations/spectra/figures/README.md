# Standalone scientific figures

These 27 editable vector figures cover the main presentation and four appendix diagrams: 26 TikZ figures and one analytic plot. Diagram labels use embedded Liberation Sans, a standard Arial-compatible sans-serif font; equations retain embedded mathematical glyphs. The presentation and standalone artwork have no company wordmark or logo.

The 26 TikZ figures are **183 × 60.047 mm**. The analytic plot is **89 × 43 mm**. Final lettering is within **5–7 pt**, and strokes are within **0.25–1 pt**. Every figure uses a white background with no raster image content. Text has not been converted to outlines. The linked Nature requirements and verification process are documented in [figure_standards.md](../../shared/figure_standards.md). Final journal layout remains an editorial decision.

## Index and figure legends

Figure numbers follow the export manifest, with figure 9 reserved for the independently generated analytic plot on slide 15. The other figure numbers follow the source-slide order; figure and slide numbers therefore differ.

1. **[The inverse problem](slide-02-inverse-problem.pdf) (slide 2).** A spectrum records peaks at mass-to-charge ratios with relative intensities. Forward fragmentation maps molecular structure to spectral evidence; the inverse problem infers a molecular graph given the spectrum and supplied formula. The left phenol and three right-hand candidate structures are explicit molecular drawings. Phenol (`Oc1ccccc1`), cyclohexa-2,4-dien-1-one (`O=C1C=CC=CC1`) and hexa-2,4,5-trienal (`O=CC=CC=C=C`) all have C6H6O, verified with RDKit. They illustrate connectivity alternatives, not candidate prevalence. The synthetic spectrum is not an experimental match to these molecules. Th denotes thomson and a.u. arbitrary units.
2. **[Two routes to identity](slide-03-two-routes.pdf) (slide 3).** The upper route maps an illustrative peak trace through an encoder and generator to a C–C–O heavy-atom graph. The lower route uses pretrained spectral features to rank supplied molecular structures, illustrated by C–C–O and C–O–C (ethanol and dimethyl ether, both C2H6O). The underline denotes a schematic selection, not a measured outcome. The spectra and molecules are not experimental matched pairs. Generation may use a fingerprint or learned embedding; this summary does not imply DiffMS consumes a thresholded fingerprint at inference.
3. **[DiffMS graph diffusion](slide-04-graph-diffusion.pdf) (slide 4).** An illustrative spectrum enters a MIST-derived encoder, whose coordinate vector h(S) conditions graph diffusion. A separate binary fingerprint strip denotes pretraining supervision rather than the inference condition. The supplied C3H8O formula fixes the pictured C,C,C,O heavy-atom inventory. Symmetric four-atom matrices are synthetic snapshots in that fixed order, with zero diagonals and the displayed categorical bond key; their shades are neither probabilities nor a measured trajectory. The spectrum is not experimentally matched to the schematic structure. Implicit hydrogen counts can vary. See [DiffMS §§3.1–3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09571v2).
4. **[Autoregressive SMILES](slide-05-autoregressive-smiles.pdf) (slide 5).** An illustrative spectrum enters MIST, which supplies predicted fingerprint coordinates to an autoregressive molecular transformer. The fingerprint strip uses a synthetic white-to-black 0–1 scale, not measured model output. The formula prefix conditions sequential generation of the illustrated phenol SMILES; the brace associates the decoder with the complete sequence. The spectrum and molecule are not an experimental matched pair. Formula conditioning does not guarantee formula compliance. Implementation: [Novogaia/md4 `ar` branch](https://github.com/Novogaia/md4/tree/ar), verified commit e804f677.
5. **[Decoder comparison](slide-06-decoder-comparison.pdf) (slide 6).** Graph diffusion repeatedly refines the bond matrix for fixed heavy atoms; autoregressive sampling writes a SMILES sequence with an explicit factorized likelihood. The synthetic ethanol example uses atom order C,C,O and categorical entries 0 = no bond, 1 = single bond, 2 = double bond. The noisy matrix need not represent a chemically valid intermediate. Token arrows represent sequential generation, not measured latency. Syntax and formula require validation for the autoregressive output.
6. **[Raw-spectra scale](slide-07-raw-spectra-scale.pdf) (slide 7).** The reservoir depicts the opportunity in more than one billion public MassIVE spectra, followed by quality control and deduplication. Miniature traces in both the MassIVE reservoir and the DreaMS selected-corpus stack are schematic spectral-record icons, not quantitative experimental plots. The “23.5M selected” caption is rounded. DreaMS uses the selected GeMS-A10 corpus of 23,517,534 spectra and masked-peak/retention-order supervision. Our branch emphasizes acquisition metadata and frozen-teacher latent targets. The branches have different corpus selections; the reservoir size is not a claim that our reported checkpoint trained on one billion spectra. Sources: [MassIVE MSV000091795](https://massive.ucsd.edu/ProteoSAFe/dataset.jsp?task=c4f2a2160dbd47148d703d2f519a103a), [DreaMS](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-025-02663-3), and [GeMS-A10](https://dreams-docs.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/msdata.html).
7. **[Acquisition-conditioned encoding](slide-08-acquisition-metadata.pdf) (slide 8).** Peak measurements and the explicit metadata-coordinate tuple (a1, a2, …, a28) feed the encoder. Those 28 encoded dimensions include measurements, presence indicators and categorical encodings, not 28 independent physical quantities. Metadata modulates normalized internal features with learned shifts, scales and residual gates; the displayed equation omits gates. The synthetic spectrum is illustrative. Output bars denote feature vectors, not quantitative values.
8. **[Two-stage pretraining](slide-09-two-stage-pretraining.pdf) (slide 9).** The visible-peak spectrum enters an MAE, which predicts a histogram over masked mass bins. Its eight illustrative probabilities are 0.02, 0.03, 0.08, 0.25, 0.30, 0.15, 0.05 and 0.12, summing to one. After the stage transition labeled “freeze,” teacher target-coordinate arrays supervise a student/predictor through MSE; that comparison does not update the teacher. The trace shares the pre-mask intensity normalization used for the full and context spectra in figure 11, and hidden peaks are omitted rather than marked at their masses. Trace, histogram and tile shades are synthetic; the grids display representative feature coordinates rather than the full latent dimensionality, and similar target/prediction shades are not an empirical result. The MAE cross-entropy is expanded into the multiscale objective in figure 10.
9. **[Analytic learning speeds](fig-09-dynamics.pdf) (slide 15)** ([live-text SVG](fig-09-dynamics.svg)). Exact normalized encoder–predictor mode progress ui/sigma_i in an aligned, balanced two-layer linear model. All modes start at fraction rho=0.015, with eta=1 and predictive singular values sigma=1, 0.55 and 0.2. Solid, dashed and dotted curves denote stronger, intermediate and weaker context-to-target links. The single vertical guide marks t=6, where progress is approximately 99.96%, 91.80% and 14.37%. The slide’s bars show these same normalized fractions, not retrieval scores. The vertical axis is dimensionless; optimization time is in arbitrary units. This is an analytic illustration, not transformer telemetry or an encoder-only downstream score, with no empirical samples, replicates or error bars. Independent mode dynamics require centered, whitened inputs, aligned initialization, balanced factors and equal rates. The singular values describe context–target cross-covariance strength, which also depends on target scale; they are not a measure of chemical relevance. Appendix C gives the ODE; the exact solution uses ui(0)=0.015 sigma_i. See the supplied analysis and [Saxe et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120).
10. **[Multiscale MAE](slide-10-multiscale-mae.pdf) (slide 10).** Masked queries predict one fine-bin distribution. Coarse probabilities sum neighboring fine probabilities. The illustrative fine masses are 0.02, 0.03, 0.04, 0.06, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20, 0.15, 0.10, 0.06, 0.05 and 0.04; coarse cells sum groups of two and four. A common white-to-black probability scale applies to every row. These probabilities sum to one and are synthetic, not training output. Intensity is an input, not an active reconstruction target in the cited recipe.
11. **[Frozen-teacher JEPA](slide-11-frozen-teacher-jepa.pdf) (slide 11).** The teacher receives the full synthetic spectrum; circles identify the two target peaks whose feature rows are selected. Those peaks are absent from the student context: their masses are not indicated by placeholders or query values. Full and context traces share pre-mask intensity normalization, also used in figure 8, so surviving peaks are not rescaled after masking. The student outputs a feature-coordinate strip Z, and the predictor returns target-coordinate arrays for MSE comparison. All traces and tile shades are illustrative rather than measured model values; feature strips and target/prediction grids show representative coordinates, not the complete latent dimensionality. The comparison does not update the frozen teacher. The cited implementation uses identity target normalization and no EMA; only student and predictor parameters are optimized.
12. **[Different codes, identical predictions](slide-12-representation-coordinates.pdf) (slide 12).** For one fixed complete input, a frozen teacher supplies the illustrative scalar target 4. One possible student code is 2 with a predictor multiplying by 2; another is 8 with a predictor dividing by 2. Both give the same final prediction. The rows show alternative factorizations, not consecutive steps or measured activations. Values are arbitrary feature coordinates, not masses or intensities. The loss constrains the encoder–predictor composition, leaving possible freedom in the code; freezing alone does not guarantee a different or better representation. The spectrum is schematic. The general invertible-coordinate argument and its model-class assumptions appear in Appendix I.
13. **[Predicting from partial evidence](slide-13-predictable-signal.pdf) (slide 13).** One synthetic visible spectrum and fixed query Q admit three equally likely complete spectra. Visible peaks share identical mass and intensity values; the dashed gray peak is withheld, varies within the same ordinal slot, and has no marker in the visible context. All four traces share scales. The same frozen teacher maps the three different complete inputs to one illustrative learned feature of 8, 10 or 12 arbitrary units. It does not give different outputs for an identical full input. An unrestricted squared-error predictor given C,Q therefore has conditional-mean optimum 10. Branches describe possible completions, not additional student inputs or a generation step. Feature values are not mass or intensity; predictability does not establish chemical relevance or student superiority.
14. **[How the predictor directs feedback](slide-14-encoder-gradient.pdf) (slide 14).** A toy code z=(1,5) enters the fixed scalar predictor g(z)=2z1, giving prediction 2 against fixed teacher target 4. With L=one-half times squared error, the direct code gradient is (−4,0). Subtracting that gradient increases z1; the unused second coordinate has no direct gradient in this example. The return path carries error feedback through the predictor, not an update to the frozen teacher. Code values are illustrative. The predictor is held fixed while code coordinates are treated as independent variables; real shared encoder parameters can move other directions, and the predictor also learns. Appendix I supplies the general Jacobian and encoder-update equations.
15. **[Representation diagnostics](slide-16-representation-diagnostics.pdf) (slide 16).** These are proposed tests, with no measured outcomes. A schematic sparse spectrum passes through a frozen encoder to a representative feature-coordinate strip; neither trace heights nor tile fills encode observed results. Compare the checkpoint's JEPA predictor with a fresh standardized retrieval readout. Across-spectrum covariance and effective rank assess representation variation. The depth/rate grid specifies separate pretraining ablations: predictor depth and predictor-to-encoder learning-rate ratio are varied independently, with fixed masks and matched compute. Grid circles denote proposed experimental settings, not samples, scores or error bars; no numerical hyperparameter levels are specified.
16. **[Single and pair states](slide-17-peak-pair-representations.pdf) (slide 17).** A single state stores a vector per peak; a pair state stores a vector for each ordered pair. Uniform pair cells are vector containers, not a scalar heatmap. The m/z values and absolute 18 Th gaps are illustrative input relations rather than established chemical assignments. Single states can already contain relational context; pair states make relations explicit.
17. **[Triangle updates](slide-18-triangle-interactions.pdf) (slide 18).** Relations involving a third peak update the i,j pair. The downward arrow denotes computational aggregation, not physical fragmentation. With signed differences defined as m_i−m_j, the displayed consistency identity uses subtraction to match the i,k and j,k edges. Gates, masks and normalization are omitted from the schematic update. Inspired by [AlphaFold2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-03819-2); peak indices do not denote residues or spatial coordinates.
18. **[Pair-distogram learning](slide-19-pair-distogram.pdf) (slide 19).** Rows and columns of the masked grid index peaks; masking a peak hides its incident pair relations. Triangle encoding and a pair predictor supervise distributions over absolute m/z gaps at multiple resolutions. The eight synthetic probability bars are 0.02, 0.03, 0.08, 0.25, 0.40, 0.15, 0.05 and 0.02, which sum to one. The vertical axis is probability, with ticks at 0, 0.2 and 0.4; the horizontal bins are schematic and do not imply eight production bins. The implemented fine head has 2,000 bins at 0.5 m/z-unit spacing. This separate research direction is not part of the single-stream system behind 33% retrieval.
19. **[Retrieval encoders](slide-20-retrieval-encoders.pdf) (slide 20).** The spectrum passes through frozen single-stream JEPA and a trainable readout; the phenol graph passes through a trainable three-layer GINE. The separate branches produce peak and atom feature arrays that both feed a peak-by-atom compatibility matrix, T = Q A_G transpose + B. JEPA provides 127 peak slots of width 2,048, mapped by the readout to width 512; atom features also have width 512. Displayed feature cells are coordinate containers and schematic subsets, not measured values or full dimensionality. Phenol has seven heavy atoms, matching the seven pictured atom rows and compatibility columns; only five representative peak rows and four feature columns are drawn. The 0–1 a.u. grayscale matrix is symbolic, not a probability normalization, a bound on real scores or a retrieval result. The synthetic spectrum and phenol are not an experimental matched pair. Padded peaks and atoms are masked; only JEPA is frozen, and no pair-stream input contributes to this historical result.
20. **[Local compatibility](slide-21-local-compatibility.pdf) (slide 21).** Rows index spectral peaks and columns index candidate atoms. This is the same five-by-seven synthetic compatibility matrix shown in figure 19 / slide 20; its seven columns represent the phenol example's seven heavy atoms. White circle markers identify each row maximum. The 0–1 a.u. color key describes the illustration, not a universal bound on model scores. A soft retained-mass bonus is added before the two directional maxima. Candidate-conditioned peak weights use the entropy of the softmax of best-atom scores over candidates. The displayed positive floor applies to pools with more than one candidate. The implementation also stabilizes the entropy logarithm and denominator, clips numerical negative information values, and normalizes across valid peaks; singleton pools receive uniform valid-peak weights. Peak-to-atom evidence is weighted; atom-to-peak evidence is uniformly averaged. Matches are scoring features, not verified fragment assignments.
21. **[Retrieval training](slide-22-retrieval-training.pdf) (slide 22).** The four schematic stages are contrastive warmup, candidate evidence, hard-negative mining and retained-mass agreement. A dark matrix diagonal marks paired positives; equal candidate compatibility makes a peak less discriminative than concentrated compatibility. The 32-to-8 illustration denotes mining up to 32 sampled negatives and retaining up to eight, always alongside the positive. The retained-fraction markers illustrate the gap between a measured peak/precursor ratio and a candidate graph fraction; they are not measurements. JEPA remains frozen, and stages initialize from validation-selected predecessors. Exact protocol: `docs/original_flare_recipe_math.md`.
22. **[Retrieval result](slide-23-retrieval-result.pdf) (slide 23).** Latest Global context + GLACIER test results: Hit@1 36.94%, Hit@5 67.07%, Hit@10 78.61%, Hit@20 88.89%, MRR 0.50814, mean MCES@1 6.14830, median rank 2 on 17,556 spectra. The headline and aligned metric table show only the latest result. Validation-selected, single-seed formula-constrained retrieval with truth included. Source: `research/latest_retrieval_result.md`.
23. **[Research directions](slide-24-research-directions.pdf) (slide 24).** Generation, representation learning and retrieval routes. The solid path connects frozen JEPA features to the latest global-context + GLACIER matcher and 36.94% test Hit@1. The dashed pair-model path remains a proposed downstream test, not the source of this result. Fingerprints and triangle states are schematic.
24. **[Gradient flow and local metric](slide-26-gradient-flow-metric.pdf) (slide 26).** Encoder and predictor contributions to residual decay are shown for fixed masked examples and unregularized continuous gradient descent. The local differential relation dŷ = Jg dz induces the positive-semidefinite metric Gz = JgᵀJg. Coordinate strips are symbolic, not measured values. Jacobians may vary with time; the decomposition is not an exact gradient-norm attribution for AdamW, momentum or weight decay.
25. **[Linear factorization](slide-27-linear-factorization.pdf) (slide 27).** The two linear operations W and P connect context, latent and predicted-target coordinate strips. Strips display representative coordinates rather than measured amplitudes or fixed input/output dimensions. Centered data and whitened context give the displayed Frobenius objective. The independent-mode and logistic solutions require singular-vector alignment, equal learning rates and balanced factors. The rank-d statement concerns the optimum of this idealized linear model, not an unconditional guarantee for transformer training.
26. **[Auxiliary readout](slide-28-auxiliary-readout.pdf) (slide 28).** Profiling the regularized objective over predictor parameters gives the Schur-complement Hessian at a differentiable inner optimum with a positive-definite predictor block. The lower vector path illustrates a proposed weak auxiliary readout of the deployed embedding. It is not a measured experiment or part of the reported retrieval recipe; a universal readout may instead invert an unstructured code. The feature strip is a symbolic coordinate container.
27. **[Retrieval details](slide-30-retrieval-details.pdf) (slide 30).** Three rows show the atom-specific soft mass bonus, candidate-dependent entropy weighting and test Hit@1. Solid fraction marks and the dashed observed-peak fraction are schematic; the nearest-fraction arrow illustrates the minimum-gap operation. Fractions are dimensionless, and the candidate fractions come from incident bridge cuts and the intact graph, with up to six retained values per atom. The probability glyphs are synthetic and share a vertical scale of 0.45 drawing units per probability one: their values are (1/3, 1/3, 1/3) and (7/90, 8/9, 1/30). Both sum to one. Lower entropy produces higher relative peak weight. Weights are normalized across valid peaks with numerical floors and a singleton-pool stabilizer; both directional matching terms retain coefficient one-half. The test statistic uses exact target-SMILES ranking over 17,556 spectra. The mass agreement remains a heuristic, not exhaustive fragmentation.

## Rebuild

From the repository root, using its existing virtual environment:

```sh
.venv/bin/python presentations/spectra/make_dynamics_figure.py
.venv/bin/python presentations/spectra/export_figures.py
```

Each TikZ PDF is paired with its editable `.tex` file. The common preamble is `nature-figure-preamble.tex`. Derived preview images, logs and contact sheets remain under `../build/figures/`; `production_audit.json` records the delivered PDF measurements.
